01 — Plumbing

One inlet manifold. Four outlets. No hidden taps.

Fees arrive from two places. They land in one manifold, and the manifold has exactly four ways out — all of them pointing back inside. There is no fifth pipe, and the engine will not settle an epoch unless the four add up to 100.00%.

03 — Proof

"Trust me" is not a mechanism.

Every epoch publishes a Merkle root over the claim set. Anyone can rebuild the tree from the published claim file, check the root matches, and check their own drip is in it — without asking us for anything.

  1. 1

    Collect

    Adapters read the fee vaults and record every finalized credit inside the epoch's slot window. Adapters can only observe — they hold no keys and can move nothing.

  2. 2

    Route

    The total is split by the policy using largest-remainder allocation, so the four buckets sum to the input exactly. No lamport is lost to integer division.

  3. 3

    Weigh

    Holder balances are integrated across the epoch — balance multiplied by slots held. A one-slot borrow earns a one-slot share.

  4. 4

    Prove

    Claims are hashed into a Merkle tree with domain-separated leaves, and the root is published. Your proof is a handful of hashes; verifying it is a loop, not an API call.

faucet — verify

Domain-separated leaves

Leaf hashes are prefixed 0x00, internal nodes 0x01. Without that, a 64-byte "leaf" can be replayed as an internal node and a proof forged for a claim that was never allocated.

No duplicated orphans

An odd node at a level is promoted, never paired with itself. Self-pairing lets one proof validate two different indices — a classic way to claim twice.

Indexed claims

The claim index is inside the leaf hash, so a claim contract can mark index n spent in a bitmap and be certain no second leaf shares it.

Dust is carried, never kept

Anything the drip can't divide evenly rolls into the next epoch's collected total. It is not burned by rounding and it is not quietly retained.

05 — The Run

Laid out like a pipe run.

Each valve opens when the one before it is proven, not when a date arrives.

  1. Manifold built shipped

    Collect / route / distribute engine, largest-remainder allocation, Merkle claims, conservation assertions, and a test suite that tries to make it leak.

  2. Dry runs against synthetic epochs shipped

    faucet cycle --epochs 6 settles a full multi-epoch run with carry-forward, and every epoch passes the conservation check.

  3. Launch on Pons next

    Mint, set the creator fee vault, point the adapters at it. faucet doctor has to come back clean before the first live cycle runs.

  4. Claim front-end

    Connect a wallet, the page fetches your proof from the published claim file and submits it. No backend, no allowlist, no form.

  5. Time-weighted index

    Replace the largest-holders floor with a streamed balance index so weighting covers every wallet, not just the top accounts.

  6. Hand over the wheel

    Move the routing policy on-chain so changing the split requires a transaction anyone can see coming, not a commit.

06 — Service panel

Questions, answered plainly.

Does "all fees go back into the project" really mean all of them?

It means every basis point of collected fees is routed to one of four destinations that the project or its holders own: the burn address, the holder drip, protocol-owned liquidity, and an on-chain build fund. There is no team bucket and no outbound wallet that isn't named on this page. Network transaction fees paid to validators to make those transfers are unavoidable and come out of the build fund.

What stops the split from being changed quietly?

Right now: the split lives in a single committed file, and this page renders the same numbers the engine settles with, so a change shows up in both places at once and in the commit history. That is honest but it is still a commit. The last item on the run is moving the policy on-chain so a change costs a visible transaction instead.

Why time-weighted balances instead of a snapshot?

Snapshots are trivially gamed. Borrow a large balance, hold it for one slot across the snapshot, claim, repay. Integrating the balance over the whole epoch makes that attack cost the full epoch instead of one slot, which is the point.

What happens in an epoch with barely any volume?

Nothing settles. If the collected total is under the settle floor, spending 0.005 SOL in transaction fees to move 0.002 SOL is a net loss, so the whole amount is carried into the next epoch and the receipt records it as carried rather than spent.

Is the drip automatic or do I have to claim?

You claim. A push distribution to thousands of accounts costs more in transaction fees than most drips are worth, and it fails on accounts that can't receive. A Merkle claim means the cost falls on whoever wants the money, when they want it, and unclaimed drips stay claimable rather than expiring.

What is actually live right now?

The engine is real, tested and runs end to end; the numbers on this page come from synthetic epochs generated by deterministic mock fee sources, because the token has not launched yet. The moment the mint and fee vault addresses go into the config, the same code runs against real vaults and this page renders real epochs. The ledger labels its source either way.

Is this financial advice?

No. It's plumbing. This is an experimental crypto project with no promise of return, no guarantee of liquidity, and a real chance of going to zero — that risk is not removed by any of the mechanics on this page. Nothing here is investment advice or an offer of anything. Read the code before you touch it.
