Every creator reward buys the coin back and burns it.

Robinhood is a token launched on Pons, on Robinhood Chain. Pons holds the creator rewards from every trade in its fee escrow. Every three minutes the engine claims them, keeps a small gas reserve, and spends everything else buying the token, with the buy delivering the tokens straight to the burn address. They never sit in a wallet anyone controls.

Every claim and every burn is a public transaction. This page renders the ledger the engine wrote.

Mechanism

One inlet, one outlet, no wallet in between.

Pons credits creator rewards, in ETH, to the dev wallet's balance in its fee escrow. Each cycle the engine calls claim() on the escrow, reads the wallet, keeps a fixed gas reserve, and sends the rest into a single buy with the recipient set to the burn address: on the Pons bonding curve before the token graduates, on its Uniswap v4 pool after. The tokens go from the market to 0x…dEaD in the same transaction. The engine then checks the wallet moved by exactly the amount sent plus gas, and refuses to record the cycle if it did not.

Policy

One rule, fixed in code.

The routing policy is a single entry in engine/src/config.ts. The engine imports it to settle and refuses to start if it does not sum to 10,000 basis points; this page reads a generated copy. Changing it is a commit that changes both at once.

Why the buy delivers to the burn address

If tokens landed in the dev wallet first, there would be a moment where they could go somewhere else. Naming 0x…dEaD as the recipient of the buy removes that moment. The buy and the burn are one atomic transaction, on the curve and on the pool alike.

Why a slippage bound

A buy with no minimum output is an invitation to be sandwiched. The engine quotes first, sets minOut at the quote less 3%, and the market reverts rather than fill below it. The node refuses such a buy before it is even signed, so it costs nothing.

Why a gas reserve

Spending the wallet to zero would strand the next cycle. A fixed reserve stays behind, and the conservation check confirms gas came out of it, not out of the buyback.

Notes

Things worth knowing before you buy.

What "all creator rewards" means

Everything Pons credits the dev wallet in its fee escrow is claimed and, above a fixed gas reserve, spent on the token and burned. There is no team allocation from rewards and no second recipient. Gas for the claim and the buy comes out of the reserve, which is itself refilled by the next reward.

What is actually live

The engine is real and tested: a real EVM runs the claim, the buy and the burn in the test suite, and the Pons and Uniswap v4 calls were checked against Robinhood Chain by simulation. The ledger on this page starts empty and fills only with real transactions. Until the first cycle it shows zero, and that is the truth.

Who holds the key

The dev wallet's key is needed only to sign the claim and the buy. The engine reads it from the environment at run time, never from a file in the repository, and checks the signer matches the configured wallet before it will send.

Quiet cycles

If nothing is claimable, or the spendable balance is under the minimum, the cycle does nothing and the rewards wait for the next one. Spending most of a small reward on gas would be a worse outcome than waiting.

Before and after graduation

A Pons launch trades on its bonding curve until it graduates, then on a Uniswap v4 pool guarded by the Pons hook. The engine reads the launch record each cycle and buys wherever the token trades now. Both venues deliver to the burn address directly.

When a buy reverts

If the market moves more than the bound between quote and fill, the buy reverts. The ETH stays in the wallet, the engine reports it rather than retrying blind, and the next cycle quotes again.

Risk

This is an experimental token with no promise of value, liquidity, or return, and a real chance of going to zero. A buyback does not change that. Nothing here is financial advice. Read the code before you touch it.
