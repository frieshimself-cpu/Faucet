Every fee drips back into the project.

Robinhood is a token on Pons whose fee stream is wired back into itself. Every six hours the engine sweeps the creator vault and the pool's fees, splits the total four ways by a fixed policy, publishes a Merkle root over the holder drip, and refuses to settle if a single lamport is unaccounted for.

Mechanism

Two inlets, one manifold, four outlets.

Fees come from the Pons creator vault and from the pool's trading fees. Once per epoch the engine reads every finalized credit to those accounts inside the slot window, adds anything carried over from a previous epoch, and routes the total. The four outlets are the only outlets. The routing step runs a conservation check and throws, rather than settling, if what was routed differs from what was collected by even one lamport.

The split

Fixed in one file, read by both.

The policy is a list of four rules in engine/src/config.ts. The engine imports it to settle; this page reads a generated copy of it. Changing the split is a commit that changes both at once, and the engine refuses to start if the rules do not sum to 10,000 basis points.

    Why domain-separated leaves

    Leaf hashes carry a 0x00 prefix and internal nodes 0x01. Without it a 64-byte leaf can be replayed as an internal node and a proof forged for a claim that was never allocated.

    Why odd nodes are promoted

    Pairing the last node with itself lets one proof validate two indices. Promoting it instead removes the double-claim.

    Why the index is in the leaf

    A claim contract can mark index n as spent in a bitmap and be certain no second leaf shares it.

    Why time-weighted

    Borrowing a balance across a snapshot block costs one slot. Integrating balance over the epoch makes it cost the whole epoch.

    Notes

    Things worth knowing before you buy.

    What "all fees" means

    Every basis point of collected fees goes to one of four destinations the project or its holders own: the burn address, the holder drip, protocol-owned liquidity, and an on-chain build fund. There is no team bucket. Validator fees paid to make those transfers come out of the build fund.

    What stops the split changing quietly

    Today, a commit. The split is one file, this page renders the same file, and a change shows up in both places and in git history at once. That is honest, but it is still a commit. Moving the policy on-chain so a change costs a visible transaction is the last item on the status list below.

    What is actually live

    The engine is real, tested, and runs end to end. The token has not launched, so the epochs on this page come from deterministic mock fee sources, and the ledger says so. When the mint and fee vault addresses go into the config, the same code runs against real vaults and this page renders real epochs.

    Quiet epochs

    If an epoch collects less than 0.01 SOL, nothing settles. Spending 0.005 SOL in transaction fees to move 0.002 SOL is a net loss, so the amount carries forward and the next epoch collects it. The receipt records it as carried, not spent.

    Claiming

    You claim; the engine does not push. Pushing to thousands of accounts costs more than most drips are worth and fails on accounts that cannot receive. A Merkle claim puts the cost on whoever wants the money, and unclaimed drips stay claimable.

    Risk

    This is an experimental token with no promise of value, liquidity, or return, and a real chance of going to zero. None of the mechanics on this page change that. Nothing here is financial advice. Read the code before you touch it.
